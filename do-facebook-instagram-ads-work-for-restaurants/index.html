Do Facebook and Instagram ads work for restaurants?

Yes, when they follow four rules. Here's the setup, the math and the real numbers.

By Luis Alonso Robles, founder of Palate · Updated November 2026 · 6-min read

The short answer

Yes, Facebook and Instagram ads work for restaurants, when they follow four rules:

  1. One ad, one action. Book a table, order, or show up on a specific night.
  2. Send people straight to the booking, not to your profile.
  3. Keep posting underneath the ads, so the page people land on looks alive.
  4. Measure cost per reservation, not likes.

Done that way, LUMA Cocktail Lounge booked 80+ reservations from $1,200 in Meta ads, about $15 a table.

Most restaurant owners have tried it once. They hit "Boost post," spent a couple hundred dollars, got some likes and saw no difference on a Friday night.

So they decide ads don't work for restaurants.

The ads weren't the problem. The setup was. Here's what works, with real numbers.

The proof$1,200 in, 80+ tables out

In March 2026, LUMA Cocktail Lounge put real money behind Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) for the first time: $1,200 over one month.

Every ad sent people straight to the LUMA website to book through OpenTable. Because nothing sat in between, every one of those 80 reservations traces back to the campaign. Whatever the daily organic posts booked on their own isn't counted.

Now the math. At two guests a table and a $50 average check, 80 reservations model to roughly $8,000 in revenue, about 6.7 times the spend. Parties of ten and twenty only push that higher.

Rule 01One ad, one action

An ad that says "Come visit us!" asks for nothing specific, so it gets nothing specific.

Pick one action per campaign: book a table, order online, or show up for a specific night. When Sip n' Tell rebuilt Taco Tuesday, all of the paid media across their market pointed at that one night. That Tuesday went from $1,300 to $4,500.

Rule 02Send people straight to the booking

Every extra tap loses guests. If your ad sends people to your Instagram profile, then they have to find the link in your bio, then find the booking page, most of them are gone.

Send ads directly to the page where the action happens: your reservation page, your online ordering, or a page for the event. Shorten the path, and track what comes through it.

Rule 03Keep posting underneath the ads

When someone sees your ad, the first thing they do is check your page. If the last post is three weeks old, the ad money is wasted.

LUMA posted organic content daily the same month the ads ran. Every ad landed on a page that looked alive. Ads bring people to the door. Your page convinces them to walk in.

Rule 04Measure cost per reservation, not likes

Likes don't pay rent. The number that matters is what you paid for each table, order or guest, compared to what that guest spends.

A simple way to check any campaign:

  • Revenue per table = guests per table × average check. (2 guests × $50 = $100.)
  • Cost per table = ad spend ÷ reservations from ads. ($1,200 ÷ 80 = $15.)
  • If revenue per table is well above cost per table, the ads are working. Spend more.

What about building an audience first?

Not every campaign has to book tables on day one. Sometimes the goal is to grow the number of locals who see everything you post, so the next event fills faster.

Common mistakes we see

  • Boosting random posts instead of building a campaign around one offer.
  • Targeting only your zip code. Guests drive across town for the right night.
  • Posting the special the morning of. Plans are made 24 to 36 hours ahead.
  • Using the same tired photo. Fresh, on-brand creative made for the offer outperforms recycled posts.
  • Turning ads off after one week. The first week is how the ads learn who your guests are.

Quick answers

How much should a restaurant spend on Facebook ads?

Start with a budget you can hold for a full month, and measure cost per reservation. Smokin' Moon BBQ grew 5,100+ followers on $500 in 30 days. LUMA Cocktail Lounge booked 80+ reservations from $1,200 in one month.

Are Instagram ads or Facebook ads better for restaurants?

Run them together. Both are managed in the same Meta ads system, and the system shifts your budget toward whichever placement books more guests.

Is boosting a post the same as running ads?

No. Boosting spreads a little money behind one post. A real campaign is built around one offer and one action, with creative made for it and a direct path to booking.
