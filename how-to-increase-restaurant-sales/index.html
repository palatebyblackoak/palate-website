How to increase restaurant sales on slow nights.

Seven plays we've run for restaurants in McAllen and the Rio Grande Valley, with the real numbers behind each one.

By Luis Alonso Robles, founder of Palate · Updated November 2026 · 7-min read

The short answer

  1. Pick one night that needs help, not the whole week.
  2. Upgrade an offer you already own instead of inventing a new one.
  3. Turn the night into an event with a theme people want to show up for.
  4. Put paid ads behind that one night, pointed at one action.
  5. Time the push for 24 to 36 hours before doors open.
  6. Keep your page alive with consistent posts, so new guests trust what they find.
  7. Borrow a local voice your city already follows.

Your Saturday is fine.

It's Tuesday that hurts. Same rent, same staff, same lights on, and half the tables empty. Most owners respond the same way: a discount, a post, and hope.

That rarely works. Here's what does, step by step, from the nights we've helped fill.

Play 01Pick one night, not the whole week

"We need more sales" is too big to act on. "Tuesday does $1,300 and it should do more" is a problem you can solve.

Pull your sales by day for the last two months. Find the night with the biggest gap between what it does and what your room can hold. That's your target. Every move below points at that one night.

Play 02Upgrade an offer you already own

Sip n' Tell already had Taco Tuesday. Guests knew it. It just wasn't worth leaving the house for.

So we didn't replace it. We rebuilt it into a combo their city couldn't ignore and stacked drink specials on top. Then we made fresh creative for that offer, not recycled posts from last week.

$1,300Average Tuesday before
$4,500The Tuesday after
48 hrsLaunch to result

That's 3.4 times the revenue on a single night. The lesson: your best new offer is usually an old one, made bigger. Guests already trust it. You just have to give them a reason to come back for it.

Play 03Turn the night into an event

A special is a price. An event is a reason.

LUMA Cocktail Lounge's regular Sunday brunch averaged $1,100. The week Taylor Swift was everywhere, we built the whole room around her: a cocktail menu named after her albums and songs, decor through the space, a DJ on her catalog all day, her videos on every screen.

$1,100Regular Sunday
$5,000+Per themed brunch
Sold outBoth events

Then we ran it back in October with Drake. Same sold-out room, same numbers.

People don't share a discount. They share a night they were part of.

Your theme doesn't need a celebrity. A game day, a holiday, a local festival or a new menu drop all work. The rule: if a guest wouldn't post a photo of it, it's not an event yet.

Play 04Put paid ads behind one night

"Boosting a post" spreads a little money everywhere. It's better to put real money behind one action.

In March 2026, LUMA's first month of real ad spend, we put $1,200 into Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram). Every ad sent people straight to the LUMA site to book through OpenTable. Nothing in between.

80+Reservations from ads
$15Cost per reservation
~$8,000Est. revenue

At two guests a table and a $50 average check, those eighty reservations model to roughly $8,000, about 6.7 times what was spent. Parties of ten and twenty only push that higher.

Not ready for that budget? Start smaller. Smokin' Moon BBQ & Beer Garden spent $500 over 30 days and gained more than 5,100 new followers, about a dime each. That's 5,100 locals who now see every event you post.

Play 05Time the push

Most restaurants post the special the morning of. By then, plans are made.

For Sip n' Tell's Taco Tuesday, the stories and reshares landed 24 to 36 hours before doors opened. That's when people are deciding what tomorrow looks like. Paid media across their market was already pointed at that one night.

A simple timeline for any slow night

  • 5 to 7 days out: announce it. Start the ads.
  • 24 to 36 hours out: the big push. Stories, reshares, staff reposting.
  • Day of: one reminder, with a photo of the room getting ready.
  • The next day: post the full room. That sells the next one.

Play 06Keep your page alive

Every ad, every influencer post and every word of mouth ends in the same place: someone checking your Instagram or Facebook. If the last post is three weeks old, they move on.

In December 2025, we ran one month of full-service social media for El Taco Mañanero on Facebook and Instagram.

7.9MFacebook views
6,200New FB followers
48.9KProfile visits

That's 17,100% organic reach growth in thirty days. Consistent posting doesn't just keep you visible. It makes every other play on this list work better.

Play 07Borrow a local voice

Your city already follows certain people for where to eat. One post from them can beat a month of yours.

Howling Rabbits Beerworks did one collaboration with Freddsters: four posts, no ad budget.

200K+Total reach
20×Engagement vs. usual posts
$0Ad spend

More than 60% of that audience had never seen Howling Rabbits before. The right voice can do more than the right budget.

Your slow-night checklist

  • I know which night is my slowest, and what it does in sales.
  • I've picked one offer I already have and made it bigger.
  • The night has a theme someone would want to post about.
  • Ads point to one action: book, call or show up.
  • The big push lands 24 to 36 hours before doors open.
  • My page has fresh posts from this week.
  • I've reached out to one local creator.

Do all seven for one night. Measure it against the same night last month. Then run it back.
